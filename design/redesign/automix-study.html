What the reference mixes do that v2 doesn't

Both clips are recordings of an 8-bar transition editor playing a finished mix. I measured their audio, read the automation curves frame by frame, and compared them with what DjComposer would write for the same pairs. The references are simpler than v2. They do fewer things, do them earlier, and line the incoming track's arrival up with the outgoing track's phrase.

Summary

Five things both clips do

The incoming arrives on a phrase line

Clip A: I KNOW?'s beat and bass hit exactly on bar 8, which is where The Hills ends. Clip B: One More Time's full low end arrives on bar 4, the window's midpoint.

The incoming is at full volume from bar 0

Neither clip fades the incoming track in. All separation happens with EQ, and with one short volume move on the outgoing deck.

Only one bass at a time

Clip A overlaps over a beatless outro, so there's nothing to clash. Clip B kills the outgoing low end on bar 0, then ramps the incoming low end in over four bars.

The outgoing leaves early and plainly

Clip B fades Starships out over the half bar before bar 4, so bars 4–8 are the new track alone. No echo, no roll, no gate in either clip.

The incoming is conformed to the outgoing's tempo, and stays there

After the window, I KNOW? plays at about 113.9 BPM (native 118) and One More Time at about 124.7 (native 126). The song you're already hearing keeps its speed.

Clip A · outro over intro

The Hills → I KNOW?

The Hills' last bass hit ends just before the window opens. For eight bars, its beatless outro and I KNOW?'s beatless intro play together at full volume. The only automation is on The Hills' highs: cut on bar 0, brought back to unity by bar 4, which makes room for the incoming intro's top end as it enters. The Hills then plays to its own ending, and I KNOW?'s beat drops on that exact bar.

What the measurements show

  • Bass and sub sit 20–60 dB down for the whole window, then jump by about 15 dB in the half bar starting at bar 8. That's I KNOW?'s drop.
  • The high band falls 13 dB over bars 0–2 and recovers by bar 3. That matches the high-EQ curve (white line) on the outgoing deck.
  • Stereo width collapses at bar 8 (side/mid ratio from about −3 dB to −13 dB), which is consistent with a mono kick and bass entering.
  • Eighth-note onsets after the window are 0.263 s apart, a grid of 113.9 BPM. I KNOW? has been slowed about 3.5% to sit at The Hills' tempo, and it stays there.

What v2 would do with this pair

  • 118 / 113 is a 4.4% difference. That's over MAX_STRETCH = .04, so matched is false and every blend family is skipped.
  • 5A → 7B is two Camelot steps, so clash() returns true. The penalty is the same whether the overlap is a dense chorus or an ambient outro.
  • What's left are the cuts and Intro wash. With a beatless intro, Intro wash likely wins. It muffles the incoming (LPF .62), builds a rising high-pass on the outgoing, throws it into an echo on bar 8, and lets that echo ring for two more bars at 113 BPM over a 118 BPM beat.
  • Every non-blend family reserves a two-bar tail (i + length + tail ≤ lastBar + 1), so the drop can never land on the outgoing's actual last bar. The Hills' ending would be cut off.

Clip B · handoff by the midpoint

Starships → One More Time

On bar 0, Starships loses its low end and its highs. Starships fades out over the half bar before bar 4. The second half of the window is the new track alone.

What the measurements show

  • Sub drops 31 dB on bar 0 (Starships' low end killed), then climbs steadily. Bass rises from −32 to −12 dB across bars 0–3.5, a straight ramp.
  • On bar 4, sub jumps another 21 dB, mids fall 3–5 dB and width narrows. Starships is gone and One More Time is at full strength.
  • From bar 4 onward every band is flat to within 2 dB. Nothing else happens in the second half.
  • Onsets after bar 4 give 124.7 BPM. One More Time (126) has been slowed to Starships' 125 and held.

What v2 would do with this pair

  • The tempos match (0.8%) and the keys don't clash, so a blend is chosen. Which blend depends on noise: Bass swap, Beat under the vocal, Filter blend and Hi-hat tease start within about a point of each other, while the seed jitter adds up to 1.5 and repeating the last family costs 1.2.
  • Bass swap, as built: the incoming is high-passed with its low end at zero, then the bass swaps instantly on bar 4.
  • The outgoing keeps some or all of its mids for bars 4–8 and leaves by one of three exits (echo, fade or high-pass) picked by the seed. A fill is picked at random too: roll, gate, drop-out, echo throw or none.
  • Starships, the track you're already hearing, is sped up 0.8% for the whole mix so that One More Time can stay native.

Proposal

Changes to v2

Add two clean families that reproduce the references, make them the default, and stop letting noise choose between candidates. The current families stay available as a Creative style.

1 · Overlay

DjComposer: new Family.OVERLAY

Used when the outgoing's last bars have no drums or bass, or the incoming's intro has none. Length L is the incoming intro length snapped to 4, 8 or 16 bars. The window is placed so that bar L is both the incoming's first full bar and the outgoing's last bar.

  • Incoming: full volume, all bands at unity from bar 0. No filters.
  • Outgoing: highs to zero on bar 0, back to unity by L/2. Plays to its own end. No echo, no tail.
  • Allowed with up to 6% tempo difference, because neither side has a beat during the overlap.

2 · Handoff

DjComposer: new Family.HANDOFF

Used for beat-matched pairs where both sides have drums. The key moment is L/2, placed on an outgoing phrase line and on the incoming's first full bar.

  • Bar 0: outgoing low and high to zero. Incoming at full volume, highs at unity, mids at 0.4.
  • Bars 0 to L/2: incoming low ramps linearly to unity, mids ease up to unity.
  • L/2 − ½ bar to L/2: outgoing volume to zero. Bars L/2 to L: incoming only.
  • On bars where both vocals are present, incoming mids stay at 0.4 until L/2 (keeps v2's clash check).

3 · Conform the incoming

DJPlanner.Plan.outgoingSpeed, Mix.speed, CrossfadePlayer

Play the incoming at the outgoing's tempo from its first sample and leave the outgoing untouched. After the outgoing has gone, hold the matched tempo (as both clips do). If the hold is more than 3% from native, glide back to native over 32 bars, which is about 0.1% per bar and inaudible with nothing to compare against. Raise the blend limit from 4% to 6% for Overlay only.

4 · Score the landing, not the noise

DjComposer.compose, entryScore

  • New landing term: +2.5 when the incoming's first full bar (drums ≥ .25 and energy ≥ .9 × body) falls on an outgoing 8-bar phrase line at or after its outro start; +3.0 when it falls on the outgoing's last bar; −1.5 when it lands mid-phrase.
  • Cap seed jitter at 0.25 for every family. It should only break ties.
  • Apply the variety penalty to cut families and fills only. A DJ who handoffs ten times in a row is doing it right.
  • Scale the key-clash penalty by how tonal the overlap is: the larger of the two decks' mean sustained chroma over the overlapping bars (from ChromaEnvelope). A beatless, sparse outro scores close to zero.
  • Let Overlay candidates end exactly at lastBar + 1, with no tail.

5 · Clean by default

PlaybackSettings, build(), applyFill

A new Mix style setting: Clean (default) allows Overlay, Handoff and Bass swap with no fills and the plain fade exit. Creative enables everything v2 has today: fills, echo and filter exits, Hi-hat tease and the cut families. Cuts remain the fallback in Clean when tempos or grids can't support a blend, using Filter fade only.

Method and limits

Audio was taken from each clip with ffmpeg (AAC, 44.1 kHz, about 66 kbps) and analysed with a 4096-point STFT. The window position comes from tracking the green playhead at 10 fps: it moves 26.2 px/s in clip A and 27.1 px/s in B, and the window takes 16.9 s and 15.4 s, which is exactly 8 bars at 113 and 125 BPM. Tempo after each window comes from eighth-note onset spacing.

The editor doesn't label its lines. Reading them as blue = volume, yellow = low EQ, pink = mid EQ and white = high EQ, with the lane centre at unity, agrees with every band measurement above. The v2 diagrams are read from the code with the clips' BPM and keys, not produced by running the planner. That needs the full tracks in the bench.

Two clips are a small sample, both 8 bars and both on beat-matched or beatless material. They don't show how the reference handles 16-bar blends, vocal-over-vocal pairs or large tempo gaps.
