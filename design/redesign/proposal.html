Design proposal · petalOS · PetalMusic v2 (bridgefx²)

Petal Music, redrawn around the seam

21 Nov 2026Scope: Android app UI (rom + fdroid), desktop parity afterStatus: for review

Petal already does something Apple Music, Spotify, TIDAL and SoundCloud can't: it owns one queue across every source and mixes between any two tracks. The current UI hides that. This proposal rebuilds the interface around big cover art and frosted glass, uses the conventions people already know from those apps, and gives Petal its own identity by making the transition between songs (the seam) the thing you see.

Everything below is based on the Compose code in app/src/main/kotlin/.../ui. The screenshots in design/review predate it (rose accent, four tabs) and were not used as the baseline.

01 · Where the app is today

What the code shows

Navigation is lopsided

Three tabs: Listen now, Search, Queue (AppRoot.kt PetalNavBar). Library has no tab. It lives inside Home as source chips, and the same HomeScreen composable is mounted for both the home and library routes. Settings hides behind a gear.

The library is organised by service

LibraryEnvironment switches the whole page per source (TIDAL, Device, Spotify, YT Music). You can't see all your albums at once, which works against the app's biggest strength.

Colour has no system

118 hard-coded Color(0x…) literals, 69 distinct, across the UI package. Card, nav, mini-player and search field each use a different near-black (1D1D21, 191B23, 29282D, 242428). The search focus ring is still the old rose FF647C. All five source palettes are copies of one violet.

The player is crowded at the bottom

Six transport icons in one row (shuffle, previous, play, next, repeat, repeat-one) plus AutoMix and favourite squeezed into the title row. Queue is opened from a “more” icon.

AutoMix is described, not shown

The best piece of UI in the app, MixWaveform, only appears during a blend. Before that, the mix point is a line of small text. The queue shows nothing about how tracks will join.

Several AI-era tells

A sparkle icon (AutoAwesome) for AutoMix, a violet-to-purple gradient on buttons, a neon edge glow during mixes (ChromaticAura), letter-spaced eyebrows (“JUMP BACK IN”), and filler copy such as “All your music. One little world.”

Type is a single face at nine inline sizes

Rubik everywhere, with 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 22 and 30sp set inline instead of through PetalTypography.

Good bones to keep

Artwork-driven player colour, the incoming cover rising through the outgoing one during a mix, the tall-touch seek bar, swipe-down to dismiss, spring transitions, source badges, the Ribbon logo pack.

02 · Direction

Borrow the conventions, own the seam

People switching from another player should find everything where they expect it: three tabs, a floating mini-player, a full-screen player with big artwork, a library with filter chips. None of that needs inventing.

The look is art first. Covers are as large as each screen allows and often run edge to edge. Everything that floats over them is frosted glass, so the app takes its colour from whatever you're looking at or listening to.

Petal's identity comes from what only Petal does. Every place two songs meet gets a visible seam: a notch on the seek bar where the mix will start, a joint between rows in Up Next that tells you how the tracks will blend, and the two-lane waveform during the blend itself. The seam is drawn with the crossfade glyph and coloured with the Ribbon dye. Nothing else in the UI uses the dye as a fill except the play button, so the seam always reads as the special thing.

03 · Foundations

Colour

Use the five palettes from the Ribbon logo pack as dyes. A dye sets the accent for chrome (play button, seam, selected states). Orchid is the default because it matches the launcher icon. A sixth option, System, takes the petalOS Material You seed. On the player, the sleeve's colour still takes over the background, as it does today.

Rule: the dye is never a gradient. accentBrush goes away. Gradients stay only where they come from the artwork (sleeve colour fading into the page).

Glass and artwork

Cover art is the largest thing on every screen that has one. Home opens on a full-bleed sleeve, album pages bleed their cover under the status bar, the player sits on a blurred, enlarged copy of the sleeve, and Library defaults to a grid of big covers.

Everything that floats over that art is frosted glass: the tab bar and mini-player, buttons laid over covers, the Up Next sheet and the player's bottom controls. Because the glass picks up the colour of whatever is under it, each album tints the whole app as you browse. Three glass levels, so the material stays consistent:

Controlblur 16 · white 13% · 1dp edge 14%
Buttons and chips on art
Barblur 24 · night 52% · top edge 7%
Tab bar, mini-player dock
Sheetblur 30 · night 50% · edge 12%
Up Next, menus, dialogs
  • Glass is only used where something sits over artwork or scrolling content. Plain lists stay on the page without a panel, so the glass keeps its meaning.
  • Each glass level has a minimum tint, so text on it passes contrast even over a white cover.
  • In Compose, real backdrop blur needs the Haze library (dev.chrisbanes.haze), backed by RenderEffect on Android 12+. Below that, or with battery saver on, the same surfaces fall back to an 88% solid tint. petalOS gets full blur. The fdroid build gets the fallback on older devices.
  • Blurred sleeve backgrounds are rendered once per cover at low resolution and cached, so they don't re-blur every frame.

Type

Three bundled faces, all SIL Open Font License, so they can ship in the ROM. One display face with character for titles, a plain workhorse for lists, and a monospace for the numbers music people actually read: BPM, key, sample rate, mix times.

Every size goes through PetalTypography. No inline fontSize in screens.

Shape and space

  • Sleeves are 4dp (6dp on the player). Square covers read as records, the way TIDAL and Apple draw them. Artists stay circular.
  • Lists sit on the page, with no card behind them. Solid cards are kept for the mix card only. Floating things are glass.
  • 10dp for the mini-player and cards, 22dp top corners for sheets, pill for chips and toggles, circles for glass icon buttons. The 1dp light edge is kept, but only on glass, where it marks the edge of the material.
  • Spacing on a 4dp grid: 16 page gutter, 12 between rows, 24 between sections.

Icons

Replace mixed Material filled icons with one stroke set (1.75dp, round caps), drawn as vector drawables. Transport icons stay filled for weight. The AutoMix sparkle becomes the seam: two crossing fade curves.

Motion

  • Keep the existing springs for navigation and the player sheet. They already feel right.
  • One signature moment: during a mix, the incoming sleeve slides in from the right over the outgoing one, and the blurred background crossfades between the two covers on the same progress value. Every glass surface on screen shifts colour with it. This replaces the neon edge glow.
  • Full-bleed covers on Home and album pages scroll at half speed and stay behind the content. Glass top bars fade in once the cover has scrolled away.
  • The seek bar morphs into the two-lane waveform when the mix starts and back when it ends (already a Crossfade; make it a height + content transform).
  • With “Remove animations” on, the sleeves swap at the midpoint with no slide.

04 · Structure

Navigation

Now
  1. Listen now: hero card, recents, plus the whole per-source library behind chips
  2. Search
  3. Queue as a tab, and again as an overlay from the player
  4. Settings: gear on Listen now
→
Proposed
  1. Home: what to play next. Quick tiles, mixes, recently added
  2. Search: every source at once, results merged
  3. Library: one library across all sources, filtered by type and source
  4. Up Next lives in the player, opened from the player or a long-press on the mini-player
  5. Settings: profile button on every tab's header

Moving Queue out of the tab bar matches every reference app and frees the slot Library needs. The Routes.QUEUE destination stays for deep links.

The mix point is always visible

  • The sleeve runs nearly edge to edge (16dp gutters) on a blurred, enlarged copy of itself, darkened just enough for white text. rememberArtPalette still sets the tint and accents.
  • Today's full-bleed crop stays available as a second style (Appearance → Player sleeve: Framed or Full-bleed). Framed is the default because it never crops the cover.
  • The bottom controls sit on a glass bar that picks up the sleeve's colour.
  • Transport drops to five controls. Repeat becomes one button cycling off / all / one, as in Apple Music and Spotify.
  • The seek bar shows the chosen cue as a notch with a dotted overlap zone, labelled mix 3:04. Today that is the “Estimated mix” text line.
  • A bottom row holds output, the seam toggle (showing the style name, tap to switch, long-press for settings), lyrics and Up Next.
  • While mixing, the header says what you're mixing into, the incoming sleeve slides over the outgoing one, the blurred background crossfades between the two covers, and the seek bar becomes MixWaveform: outgoing above, incoming below, beat grid, and the four band handovers. Band curves stop at the playhead because the controller doesn't predict the future. The readout uses the mono face.
  • Title and favourite move to the incoming track at the halfway point, matching when the audio actually hands over.

Library

One library, every source

  • Content type is the first filter, source is the second (in the sort row). This replaces the per-source TidalEnvironment / LocalEnvironment pages with one list fed by all of them.
  • Albums and playlists default to a two-column grid of large covers, with the glass dock floating over them as you scroll. The list view (toggle at the top right) stays for long collections and shows a small mono tag for each item's source. Petal playlists (local, mixing any sources) get their own tag.
  • Liked songs merges favourites from every source into one playlist.
  • Downloads gets a pinned row and a filter chip, so the separate Downloads screen becomes a filtered view.
  • A service that isn't signed in shows a single row that links to Connections, instead of a full-page sign-in state.

07 · Implementation

Order of work

Phase 1
Foundations

New ui/theme/Tokens.kt with colour roles (night/paper surfaces, text levels, dye, mix bands), PetalShape, spacing and motion specs. Rework PetalPalette into a dye + artwork palette, keeping fromArtwork. Bundle Schibsted Grotesk, Instrument Sans and IBM Plex Mono in res/font and rebuild PetalTypography. Draw the stroke icon set as vector drawables. Add Haze and a PetalGlass modifier with the three levels and the solid fallback, plus a cached low-resolution blurred-sleeve loader. Replace all 118 colour literals with tokens. No layout changes yet, so this lands safely on its own.

Phase 2
Shell

AppRoot.kt: Home / Search / Library tabs, profile button to Settings, mini-player with progress hairline and cue tick, long-press opens Up Next. Split HomeScreen so Home and Library are separate composables.

Phase 3
Player

NowPlayingScreen.kt layout, merged repeat, seam toggle, cue notch on PetalSeekBar (reads transitionStartMs), restyled MixWaveform, sleeve slide during blends. Delete ChromaticAura.kt and the unused PetalBloom.kt. QueueScreen as a sheet with seam rows. This needs a per-pair transition preview from AutomixEngine; where none exists yet, the seam row shows the style and fade length from settings.

Phase 4
Library and detail

Unified library model merging the per-source environments, Liked songs across sources, Downloads as a filter. ArtHeader.kt becomes the left-aligned header for album, artist and playlist.

Phase 5
Search and settings

Merged and de-duplicated search results (match on ISRC where the source gives it, else normalised title + artist + duration). Settings split into sub-pages.

Phase 6
Home extras, desktop

Mix card generator. Port tokens to desktop/.../ui/theme/Palette.kt, ideally by moving tokens into a shared module both targets read.

08 · Decisions needed

Questions for you

Default dye. Orchid matches the launcher icon but is loud. Twilight or Amethyst would be calmer. Or should the default be System (Material You)?
How much glass? The mockups use moderate blur with a fairly dark tint. Clearer glass shows more of the cover but needs more tint behind small text. A strength setting in Appearance is possible.
Light theme in the first release? Paper doubles visual QA. It could follow in a later release.
Three tabs or four? This proposal uses three. A fourth for Downloads would suit heavy offline use.
Mix card on Home. Ship it with the redesign, or after?
Fonts. Comfortable moving off Rubik? Bundling three families adds a few hundred KB of font files to the APK.
